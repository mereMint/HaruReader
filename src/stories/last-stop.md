# Last Stop

> **Content warning:** graphic violence, body horror, blood/gore, death, and threat.

<!-- ambient: night, neon -->

“So, are you guys planning on going to that party in K3? Some people I know invited me, and they said it’s going to be amazing. Like, *really* huge. They’re probably going to use an entire floor of that megabuilding,” Jinji says.

“You do know we have to present our project tomorrow, right?” Matzuki adjusts his glasses. “Or did you forget the part where we need a passing grade?”

“I mean, we could use a break, don’t you think, binbo boy?” Inose pats the crushed cigarette pack in his jacket pocket. “Fine. We finish the presentation first. After that, all three of us are going out. No excuses.”

“Don’t call me that.” Matzuki’s voice rises, but there is no real bite behind it.

The walk from Taka University of Science to Katoshi Garden Station takes about ten minutes. The boys spend most of it too tired to speak. When the station’s glass domes finally rise beyond the trees, Jinji breaks the silence.

“So we’re going back to the dorm, sleeping for four hours, and then returning to work?” He sighs. “That’s not how I imagined student life. I thought I’d meet girls and party all night. Instead, I’m working on algae until three in the morning.”

“Engineered algae,” Matzuki corrects him. “And our work matters.”

“Yeah,” Inose says. “If the next trial works, it could help restore entire stretches of coastline.”

“It still smells like a dead aquarium.”

“Because you keep leaving the sample containers open,” Matzuki says.

Jinji drags his feet down the station steps. “Personally, I think we’re the only team working with algae because everyone else realized how boring it is.”

“Then why are you still on our team?” Inose asks.

“Because I need a good grade...” Jinji glances away. “And maybe because I like hanging around you people.”

The last part barely escapes his lips.

For a moment, none of them says anything.

<!-- ambient: night, forest, moonlight -->

Inose almost smiles. He looks away before either of them can catch it. Without another word, he takes the lead and walks into the vast station. Glass domes stretch high above them, their steel frames slowly being reclaimed by vines that weave naturally across the structure. Moonlight filters through the glass, casting a pale glow over the otherwise dim station.

“Damn,” Inose says, spinning beneath one of the domes. “This place is beautiful at night.”

Matzuki looks toward a row of dead ceiling lights and the maintenance barriers stacked against the eastern wall. A yellow notice reads: **EAST SERVICE ROOMS — LOCK REPAIRS IN PROGRESS.**

“The lights have been broken since I started studying here,” he says. “There is a difference between beautiful and neglected.”

Jinji studies the arrival display. “Eighteen minutes? This is supposed to be one of the busiest stations in Sodeine.”

“The Taka University interchange is busy,” Matzuki replies. “Katoshi Garden serves the old botanical campus. At three in the morning, we should be grateful a train comes at all.”

“Our poor man’s Orbit,” Jinji mutters.

<!-- ambient: forest, neon, flicker -->

He wanders toward a huge glass wall enclosing rare tropical plants. Most of the display is dark, but a flickering advertisement glows between the leaves:

**TAKALABS — LIFE BEYOND LIMITS.**

Jinji leans against the glass. “At least the maglev will be empty. Apart from drunk people and students who actually went to those parties.”

“You are still talking about that?” Inose lowers himself onto the floor beside the display. “We have eighteen minutes to kill. Find a new subject.”

“Did you hear that?” Matzuki asks.

_Hehehe._

Jinji’s smile disappears. “Yeah. I heard it.”

The laughter comes again—thin, uneven, with another pitch dragging underneath it. Jinji cannot tell whether one person is laughing or several. For several seconds, there is nothing else but vines pressing against the glass and the soft electrical hum of the advertisement.

*Thonk.*

A face strikes the panel behind Jinji.

He stumbles away with a sharp breath. On the other side stands a thin man among the plants, his forehead pressed to the glass. A service hatch hangs open in the shadows behind him. Glowing yellow eyes jerk in different directions, never focusing on one thing for more than a heartbeat. He looks less like he is watching them than searching for something only he can see.

“What the hell?” Inose scrambles to his feet. “How long has he been standing there?”

“We should leave,” Matzuki says. “Now.”

“No argument from me.” Inose pulls Jinji away from the display. “We can walk to the dorm.”

Jinji remains pale, but curiosity holds him in place. “How did he get in there? What’s wrong with him?”

The man suddenly clutches his head. A scream tears out of him with a metallic rasp threaded through it. His body bends into a terrible arch. Black actuator lines rise beneath the skin of his arms before he collapses backward into the plants.

“He’s having a seizure,” Jinji says, rushing toward the glass. “We have to help him.”

“No!” Matzuki grabs his sleeve. “We don’t know what he has. Call MedTec, but don’t go near him.”

<!-- ambient: dark, danger, suspense -->

Bones crack beyond the glass. One final scream cuts through the room.

Then everything goes silent.

Jinji hammers his fist against the panel. “Hey! Can you hear me? Are you okay?”

Inose drags him back. “Are you crazy?”

Matzuki stands several steps behind them, shaking. A rumor from the university clinics returns to him: patients who stopped feeling pain, stopped recognizing the people around them, and kept moving long after their bodies should have failed.

“Spiraling,” he whispers. “So it’s true.”

He looks up at his friends. “We need to go. Right now.”

The man launches himself through the glass.

The panel explodes across the station. His body strikes the floor and lies limp among the glittering shards. Blood slowly pools beneath him.

Then one eyelid twitches.

His fingers curl against the broken glass. He lifts his head, and for the first time his yellow eyes settle on the three students.

His gaze drifts toward the TakaLabs advertisement.

“Life is relentless beauty,” he says, his voice mechanical and strangely melodic. “It shines brightest beyond the boundaries of its frame.”

He studies his blood-covered hands. “Beyond limits,” he whispers, satisfied.

“Run!” Inose shouts.

“Don’t leave me, my shining beauties!” the man calls after them.

The three students run. Something whistles through the air behind them.

Inose cries out and crashes to the floor. A scalpel is buried deep in his lower leg, leaving only a sliver of its handle exposed. Blood spreads around the wound.

Matzuki drops beside him, then recoils from the buried blade. “We can’t pull that out here. We have to move.”

“We can’t leave him,” Jinji says. He pulls Inose upright and drapes one of his arms across his shoulders. “I’ll take him. Matzu, call the HPD. Find somewhere safe.”

<!-- ambient: dark, suspense, monitor -->

Matzuki hesitates only long enough to meet their eyes. Then he runs into the short corridor serving the eastern maintenance rooms.

At its far end, one gray door marked **SECURITY** stands open by several centimeters. Its card reader has been stripped down to a nest of colored wires, and a maintenance wedge lies beneath it. A yellow tag hangs from the exposed lock assembly: **ACTUATOR REPLACEMENT PENDING — MANUAL LATCH ONLY.** Someone has left a tool bag on the floor.

Matzuki kicks the wedge away, squeezes inside, and pulls the door shut. A heavy mechanical deadbolt sits beneath the broken electronic lock. He throws it into place.

The security post runs along the platform wall, narrow and lined with eight inactive monitors. A broad one-way window overlooks most of the station. From the platform, its electrochromic outer layer displays a looping advertisement; from inside, it is meant to give security staff a clear view while the observation room remains dark.

A contact window flickers to life in his vision. With a shaking gesture, he selects the HPD emergency line.

“Come on,” he whispers. “Pick up.”

“HPD Dispatch. What is your emergency?”

“Someone is attacking us at Katoshi Garden Station,” Matzuki says. “He came through reinforced glass. I think he’s Spiraling.”

The dispatcher’s voice changes immediately. “Are you inside a secured room?”

“Yes, but my friends are still on the platform.”

“Remain where you are. We are initiating station containment to prevent the suspect from reaching the street or an arriving train. Blue refuge bays will remain unlocked.”

“You’re locking them in?”

“The refuge bays are reinforced and connected to emergency ventilation. Direct your friends to the nearest one if you can do so without opening your door. A suspected Spiraling event has also been forwarded to the national hazard channel. A response team is on its way.”

“Wait—”

The call collapses into an automated status window:

**HPD REMOTE CONTAINMENT — PERIMETER SEAL IN 90 SECONDS — REFUGE ACCESS ACTIVE.**

Matzuki drops into the chair before the dark monitors. Every angle of the station should be there, if he can wake the system.

“Please work,” he mutters.

He flicks the first switch.

<!-- ambient: dark, monitor, flicker -->

The station goes dark.

One bank of lights dies after another until only the advertisements remain, glowing on an independent emergency circuit. The containment countdown continues on its own local safety controller.

“Goddammit.”

He snaps the switch back. Nothing happens. He hits the square reset button beside it, and the security system coughs to life. The monitors wake one by one, filling the room with pale blue light.

Matzuki searches the feeds.

The first camera shows an empty stairwell. The second shows the broken glass display. On the third, the man stands alone beneath the moonlight, turning slowly as he listens to the station.

Jinji and Inose are nowhere in sight.

The man’s bare foot enters a thin stream of blood. He looks down and crouches beside it. One finger touches the glossy red line, then rises to his tongue.

“Where did you go?” he sings.

Three camera microphones catch his voice a fraction of a second apart. The words repeat across the room in broken overlaps.

Matzuki switches to the fourth feed.

Nothing.

Fifth.

Nothing but empty benches beneath the green pulse of an advertisement.

On the sixth, he finally sees them. Jinji and Inose are crouched behind an abandoned maintenance cart near the eastern wall. Jinji has one hand pressed over Inose’s mouth. Inose is biting into his palm to keep from crying out. Blood gathers beneath his injured leg and creeps beyond the cart’s wheels.

The man follows the trail.

He does not hurry. He wanders from bench to bench, looking beneath each one as if playing a game with children. Sometimes he disappears outside the cameras’ view, leaving Matzuki with nothing but the soft slap of bare footsteps through the microphones.

The footsteps stop.

On the sixth feed, the man’s shadow stretches across the tiles beside the maintenance cart.

Inose’s leg trembles. The exposed scalpel handle taps softly against the metal frame.

The man tilts his head.

_If he bends down, he’ll see them._

Matzuki runs his fingers across the security controls. Most of the labels mean nothing to him. He traces the numbered speakers against the camera map and finds one beyond the cart, farther along the eastern wall. If the man follows it, he will have to turn his back on Jinji and Inose—and pass the security window.

Matzuki strikes the corresponding button beneath **PLATFORM AUDIO TEST**.

A three-note maintenance chime rings across the eastern wall.

The man turns toward it.

His shadow withdraws from the cart. He moves toward the sound, bouncing lightly on the balls of his feet while a sickening hum slips through the camera microphones.

Then he leaves the camera’s view.

A shadow enters the one-way window beside Matzuki.

The man walks slowly across the glass, so close that Matzuki can see droplets of blood trembling on his eyelashes. The platform advertisement throws green and gold across his face. In the dark room, the monitors leave Matzuki blue.

The man stops.

His head turns toward the glass.

Matzuki freezes. The window should be opaque from the platform. The advertisement is brighter than the room, and the monitor hoods are designed to keep their light from reaching it. Still, the man’s yellow eyes settle exactly where Matzuki is sitting.

In the glass, the man’s bloody grin hangs over Matzuki’s pale reflection.

_He can’t see me. The glass is one-way. He can’t see me... right?_

The man leans closer until his forehead almost touches the advertisement. His gaze does not wander. It presses through the window and into Matzuki, as if staring past his face and directly into whatever is hiding behind it.

One blackened fingernail taps the glass.

Once.

Twice.

Then the man smiles at Matzuki’s reflection.

Behind him, one of the cart’s wheels squeaks.

His eyes snap toward the sound.

Jinji hauls Inose upright, and they stagger from their hiding place. The man turns away from the glass and follows them, still walking, as if he knows they cannot move fast enough to escape him.

Jinji has an arm locked around Inose’s waist. Inose’s injured leg drags behind them, leaving a broken red trail across the tiles.

“We’re nearly there,” Jinji pants. “Twenty-five meters, maybe. Keep fighting.”

“You’d better not drop me, idiot,” Inose says through clenched teeth. “You still owe me that night out.”

Jinji lets out something between a laugh and a sob. “Then stop bleeding on me.”

On the monitor, Jinji drags Inose toward the street stairwell. For one impossible second, Matzuki thinks they have made it. Then he sees the containment countdown above the stairs reach three.

The relief dies. They are running straight into a dead end.

He grabs the station microphone and slams the broadcast switch.

“Jin, not the stairs! Blue refuge bay to your left. Move!”

<!-- ambient: dark, danger, emergency -->

Warning lights ignite above the platform. Steel containment shutters descend across the stairwells and tunnel entrances, separating the station from the city and the active guideway.

Jinji changes direction, dragging Inose toward a blue door set into the wall.

The man steps between them and the refuge bay.

Matzuki tightens his grip on the microphone. His next words betray that someone is watching.

The refuge icon glows green on the station map. The door is open. Matzuki hits the controls anyway. The refuge stays open. The shutters stay down. Nothing on the console can move the man out of the doorway.

“I’m the one talking to you. Leave them and come find me.”

Slowly, the man lifts his face toward the nearest camera.

For ten long seconds, he stares through the feed and into Matzuki’s eyes. His yellow irises jitter once, then settle.

He smiles.

<!-- ambient: rain, danger, emergency -->

The man drives his fist through a red cabinet marked **PLATFORM DELUGE** and tears down the manual release lever. The alarm shrieks. Valves open above the platform, and water erupts from the suppression nozzles.

Water sheets across the platform. Blood runs pink between the tiles, and the polished floor turns treacherous beneath Jinji’s shoes.

The man strolls toward them through the artificial rain.

“Jin,” Inose whispers. His fingers tighten around Jinji’s shoulder. “Don’t let go.”

Jinji turns, trying to pull him away. His heel slips. For one desperate second he keeps Inose upright by strength alone; then the man seizes Inose by the hair and wrenches him backward. Jinji clings to his friend’s waist until the attacker swats him aside. He strikes a containment shutter and drops hard.

“Oh, don’t look like that,” the man says.

Synthetic muscle cables draw taut beneath the skin of his forearms. His reinforced ankle locks with a metallic click. He plants one foot hard enough to craze the tile and wrenches Inose upright by the hair.

One arm hooks beneath Inose’s jaw. The other hand braces against his shoulder.

“Heads up.”

Jinji understands the joke a heartbeat before a wet chain of cracks races down Inose’s neck.

<!-- ambient: rain, grief, emergency -->

His body collapses. His head remains in the man’s hands.

For a second, Matzuki cannot understand what the monitors are showing. One feed holds Inose’s body beneath the platform lights. Another holds his face in the man’s hand. The station has split his friend into camera angles, and Matzuki is trapped in the room that can see all of them.

Matzuki folds over the console and vomits. When he forces himself upright, Inose’s body is still twitching on one of the monitors.

He hits the refuge control again. Green. He tries the platform speakers, the deluge override, the sealed stairwell. Every system answers except the one he needs.

Jinji scrambles into the corner beside the shutter, slips in the bloody water, and vomits between his knees.

“Inose.” Jinji’s voice breaks on the name. “Give him back.”

<!-- ambient: rain, danger, emergency -->

The man looks up at the camera, Inose’s head hanging from his hand.

“You see it too, little security guard?” he asks. “Let me help you see him better.”

He hurls the head toward the camera.

“Catch!”

It strikes the wall below the lens. The feed jolts, then corrects itself. Inose’s face fills the monitor at a crooked angle while water runs over one half-open eye. For an instant, the station software draws an identification frame around him. Then the frame turns red and disappears.

The man laughs. “Oh, no. You can’t.”

He turns back to Jinji. “I’ll make your friend watch.”

“No!” Matzuki grabs the microphone again. His mouth tastes of vomit and blood where he has bitten his tongue. “Leave him alone. Please. You can take me instead.”

The man does not even glance up.

Jinji presses himself against the shutter as if he can force an exit into existence. “Stay away from me.”

The man clamps one hand around Jinji’s skull and drives him against the steel.

Jinji punches his ribs, kicks at his knees, and claws at the hand covering his face. Nothing moves him. Actuator bundles tighten inside the attacker’s forearm with a rising mechanical whine.

The shutter flexes.

Bone begins to crack.

“Stop!” Matzuki screams. “Please, stop!”

Jinji’s fists strike more slowly. His shoes scrape against the wet floor. One hand finds the seam of the shutter and grips it, as if he can hold himself in the station by his fingertips.

Matzuki keeps the broadcast switch pressed. He has no words left. The open channel carries only his breathing, the deluge, and the sound of something breaking against steel.

Jinji’s fingers loosen.

<!-- ambient: rain, grief, emergency -->

His body goes limp. When the man removes his hand, blood runs down the dented shutter. A tiny green light in Jinji’s damaged interface continues blinking against his collar, reporting a connection after there is nobody left to answer it.

For several seconds, the only sounds in the station are the deluge and the alarm.

The man wipes his hand across Jinji’s shirt. Then he looks toward the eastern service rooms and begins to hum again.

“I’m coming for you, little watcher,” he sings.

Matzuki releases the microphone.

On the monitors, the man walks toward the eastern service rooms. He disappears from one feed and appears in the next, his head tilted as if listening for movement behind the walls.

<!-- ambient: rain, suspense, monitor -->

Matzuki dims the monitors until the security room is almost black. The one-way glass becomes a field of faint reflections: eight dying camera feeds, the moonlit platform, and his own frightened eyes.

The man reaches the service corridor.

Then he disappears from every camera.

Matzuki slides from the chair and crouches beneath the console. There is nowhere else to hide. The maintenance crew has opened the lock housing, but the walls and floor remain solid concrete. The unfinished repair left the door open for him; now it has left him with only one mechanical deadbolt between himself and the platform.

Wet footsteps enter the corridor outside.

They stop at the first door.

A handle rattles. Metal groans. The door crashes open, and the man laughs when he finds the room empty.

The footsteps continue.

Second door.

Again, he tests the handle gently before using force. He is not rushing. Beyond that room, only the security post remains.

The footsteps approach Matzuki’s door—and pass it.

They fade toward the platform.

Matzuki opens the HPD status window in his vision.

**RESPONSE EN ROUTE — ESTIMATED ARRIVAL: 14 MIN 18 SEC.**

Fourteen minutes.

The deadbolt will not survive fourteen minutes.

A dark shape glides across the one-way window. The man has circled back onto the platform. He walks past without stopping.

Matzuki holds his breath until pain spreads through his chest.

For several seconds, there is only the deluge striking the platform.

_He missed me._

Matzuki slowly raises his head above the console.

The window is empty.

<!-- ambient: rain, danger, monitor -->

Then a bloody hand slaps against the other side.

Matzuki bites down on a scream.

The man rises slowly from below the window until his face fills the glass. He must have crouched beneath it and waited for Matzuki to look. The exterior advertisement flickers across his skin, turning the blood he dragged across the platform gold and green.

He places one fingertip against the exact position of Matzuki’s reflected eye.

“There you are.”

The hand slides from the glass, leaving a red print over Matzuki’s reflection.

The man slips out of sight.

Wet footsteps enter the service corridor again. This time they stop behind Matzuki.

The handle turns once.

A thread of blue monitor light escapes through the unfinished lock housing. The handle goes still. Then one yellow eye appears among the exposed wires where the card reader should be.

“Found you,” he whispers.

Matzuki drops beneath the console again.

The first impact shakes dust from the ceiling. The deadbolt jumps inside its bracket.

Matzuki clamps both hands over his mouth.

The second impact folds the metal door inward around the lock. A wet foot slides outside as the man steps back for another strike. In the narrow gap beside the frame, Matzuki glimpses blood-slick fingers curl into a fist.

The third impact bends the deadbolt bracket away from the concrete. One mounting bolt shoots across the room and skips beneath the console beside Matzuki’s knee.

The man pushes his fingers through the widening gap and feels blindly for the latch.

The HPD timer reads **13:41**.

The metal bracket tears another centimeter from the wall.

<!-- ambient: rain, danger, moonlight -->

A low vibration rolls through one of the glass domes above the station.

The man goes still.

An unmarked gray AT hovers directly over the eastern dome. A ring of shaped cutters flashes across the curved glass. Four magnetic clamps catch the severed disk before it can fall and winch it clear of the station. Moonlight pours through the opening. Four cables drop from the aircraft, and armored figures descend through the dome, boots first, their silhouettes briefly suspended against the moon.

Their helmets have no HPD markings. Each shoulder carries the same white flower emblem as the TakaLabs advertisement.

Matzuki raises his head just far enough to see over the console. Through the one-way window, he watches the squad touch down across the flooded platform.

The four figures release their cables at the same instant. Two turn outward to cover the tunnel and stairwells. A third kneels beside an external station terminal and connects a white authorization key. The last faces the security-room door just as the man pulls his fingers from the gap.

A filtered voice carries across the platform. “Confirmed enhanced hostile. Guidance boundary set. No fire beyond the platform.”

The man looks from one helmet to the next. His ruined smile widens.

Then he charges them.

One armored figure draws a compact, boxy pistol roughly the size of a machine pistol. Its short barrel cracks with a magnetic pulse. The first projectile clears the muzzle; a heartbeat later, its own motor ignites with an insect-like whine. The pistol barely moves in the figure’s hand.

The man twists aside. The projectile banks after him. He changes direction again, moving with enough force to crack a tile beneath his heel, but the round corrects with him.

Three more guided projectiles launch from the pistol. They spread across the station to avoid one another, their motors painting brief white curves through the rain before all four turn back toward the same target.

The man reaches the first squad member.

The rounds reach him a fraction of a second later.

The first round tears through the man’s jaw and ruins whatever he was about to say. The second strikes beneath his left eye; the yellow glow jerks sideways and goes dark as the guidance motor drives into his skull.

The final two rounds meet at the reinforced column in his neck from opposite directions. His head separates and strikes the containment shutter before dropping into the shallow water.

His headless body takes two more steps before collapsing.

For several seconds, nobody moves. A yellow light inside the ruined eye flickers once beneath the water and goes out.

The squad does not celebrate. One member watches the body while two others unfold a black containment bag. They scan the severed head and lock it inside a rigid specimen case. Then they roll the body onto a reinforced liner so the damaged implants cannot tear through the bag.

“Specimen temperature is falling,” one of them says.

“Preserve whatever remains of the neural lattice. Move.”

The squad member at the station terminal finishes entering the authorization code. Inside the security room, every monitor turns white.

**GOVERNMENT PRIORITY OVERRIDE — TAKA RECOVERY AUTHORITY.**

Another message replaces it:

**REMOTE EVIDENCE TRANSFER COMPLETE — LOCAL CACHE PURGED.**

<!-- ambient: rain, grief, monitor -->

Matzuki stares at the empty feeds. The only record of what happened is gone.

The helmet of the fourth squad member turns toward the one-way window. A thin sensor line passes over the glass and pauses where Matzuki is crouching.

“One live civilian inside the security post,” the squad member reports.

“HPD has the survivor handoff. Recovery remains priority.”

The containment status changes from HPD red to government-priority white. The squad attaches the sealed bag and specimen case to separate recovery cables. The body rises first, then the case holding the severed head, both disappearing through the cut opening into the AT.

Nobody calls out to the survivor.

Nobody checks Jinji or Inose.

Matzuki remains beneath the console as the four figures reconnect their harnesses and rise through the dome. The aircraft turns, its downdraft driving rain and loose leaves in circles across the station, and then accelerates into the night.

He keeps counting after the sound has disappeared. Numbers are easier than looking through the window.

At three hundred, he crawls out from beneath the console.

The door will not open at first. The impacts have warped it around the frame. Matzuki retracts the bent deadbolt with both hands, braces one foot against the wall, and pulls until the remaining bracket tears free. The door gives him a gap barely wide enough to squeeze through. A jagged edge cuts his sleeve as he enters the corridor.

<!-- ambient: rain, dark, grief -->

The station is empty except for the dead.

Matzuki crosses the platform slowly. He looks at the floor because every direction contains something he does not want to see. Inose lies where the attacker dropped him. Jinji is still folded beneath the dented shutter.

Matzuki sits between them. Water drips through his hair and runs down his face. He reaches toward Jinji once, stops before touching him, and lowers his hand.

Inose’s crushed cigarette pack protrudes from his soaked jacket.

Matzuki takes one cigarette and finds Inose’s cheap plasma lighter in the other pocket.

The lighter sparks twice before producing a small blue flame. He shields it with both hands, the way Inose always did in the wind outside the laboratory while Jinji complained about the smell.

The deluge extinguishes it almost immediately.

He holds the ruined cigarette anyway.

<!-- ambient: night, clinical, grief -->

* * *

Seven minutes later, the street-side containment shutter groans upward. Two HPD response vehicles wait beyond it. Officers and medical staff enter with weapons drawn, and a body-recovery van follows them.

One officer lowers her weapon as soon as she sees Matzuki. She approaches slowly. “I’m Officer Nakano. I’m going to put a blanket around you. Is that okay?”

Matzuki manages a nod. Only then does she touch his shoulder and wrap a silver emergency blanket around him.

A medical scanner passes over his eyes and chest. The device finds no major physical injury, but it keeps warning that his heart rate is dangerously high.

“You’re safe now,” the officer says.

Matzuki looks toward the black circle cut from the eastern dome. He does not believe her, but he does not have enough strength to argue.

“What’s your name?” she asks.

“Matzuki.”

Her gaze moves to the opening in the dome and, through the bent security-room door, the white override notice still burning across the erased monitors.

“Who else was here?”

Matzuki looks at the black space where the attacker’s body had been.

“Taka,” he whispers.

The officer’s expression tightens. She switches off her body microphone before asking her next question.

“Tell me exactly what you saw.”

Matzuki looks at his friends. “I want to go home.”

The officer does not press him. She helps him into the back of the second car and closes the door against the sound of the alarm.

As they pull away, a university reminder appears in Matzuki’s vision:

**COASTAL RESTORATION PROJECT — PRESENTATION 09:00 — JINJI / INOSE / MATZUKI.**

He dismisses it with a trembling hand.

Behind him, the damaged arrival display remains frozen at **MIKITA LINE — 04 MINUTES**, promising a train none of them will take.
